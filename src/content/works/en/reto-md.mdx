<CaseSection id="overview" title="The web presence of Reto MD and its specialty sites">

Reto MD is an app that prepares Mexican physicians for the ENARM, the national medical residency exam; under the Reto brand, Mellow also runs continuing medical education sites by specialty. From 2021 to 2023 I was the frontend developer at Mellow, the company behind Reto MD, and I made the frontend decisions for its products, working closely with the backend, design, and product teams.

Its websites showcase the product, sell memberships, and bring in new users. I built the corporate site and the network of specialty sites, and I wrote the entire codebase behind that web presence. Screenshots show the sites as of 2026, so they may include changes made after I left, and the visual design is by Mellow’s design team.

<CaseSection id="challenge" title="A lot of content, and a brand for every specialty">

The corporate site had to bring a lot together in one place: the blog, the job board, events, webinars, podcasts, information about the app and the ENARM, and an About Us section. Accounts also had to work with Firebase and with the platform’s API at the same time.

### One site per specialty

Reto was also growing by specialty. I built the Reto Dermatología, Reto Cardiología, and Reto Oftalmología websites, each in its own repository, and the network was set to keep growing until it reached six specialties. Every brand needed its own identity without starting each launch from scratch.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="approach" title="One configuration per brand on a single codebase">

I consolidated the repositories of every specialty into a multi-tenant architecture on the Next.js App Router. At its core is a configuration interface in TypeScript, where each brand declares its colors, the pages and sections it shows, and its brand vocabulary.

A specialty stopped being a separate project and became a configuration. Internationalization, built with next-intl, lives in the same codebase.

<CaseSection id="research" title="What changes from one specialty to the next">

The three sites I had built separately made it clear: from one specialty to the next, what changed were the colors, the visible pages and sections, and the brand’s words. The structure was the same.

Those three differences are the fields of the configuration. Everything else became shared code.

<CaseSection id="outcome" title="A new brand, in production in an hour">

A single repository generated six independent Vercel deployments: Cardiología, Dermatología, Oftalmología, Pediatría, Psiquiatría, and Nefrología. With the content ready, a new brand could go live in about an hour.

I wrote the entire codebase behind the web presence: the frontend implementation, the service integrations, and a shared architecture that simplifies maintenance and future launches.
